Earthquake energy · 9 slides

A hundred taps or one blow?

A goldsmith taps a hundred times. A blacksmith swings once. Earth does both: thousands of small tremors and the occasional great quake. Which one moves more energy?

1 · What magnitude measures

Each step up is about 32 times more energy

Magnitude is a logarithmic scale. Going from M7 to M8 multiplies the released energy by about 31.6. Two steps multiply it by about 1,000.

3 · The race

Two exponentials pulling in opposite directions

Going up one magnitude, the number of quakes drops 10 times, while the energy of each one rises about 32 times. The total energy of that magnitude band therefore still rises about 3.2 times.

The total rises because 32 is bigger than 10. Small quakes lose the race at every step.

6 · Try it yourself

What if small quakes were even more common?

The ratio of small to large quakes is the b-value. Real regions sit between about 0.8 and 1.2. Drag it and watch where the energy goes.

7 · The real record, 1900–2026

Five giants in 126 years

The statistics predict about 10 M9 quakes per century. The real record has 5 since 1900, so the biggest events are even rarer than a simple extrapolation suggests. They still dominate: the 1960 Chile quake alone is often estimated at roughly a quarter of all seismic energy released since 1900.

Circle area is proportional to energy. Chile's M9.5 released about 5.6 times as much energy as Tohoku's M9.0–9.1.

8 · The answer

One blow wins

Small quakes are far more common, but not common enough. Every step down in magnitude gives 10 times as many quakes, and each one carries about 32 times less energy. Added up, the small quakes fall short at every step.
